Sunburst Pop

The system reference. Arcade-cabinet joy, built on one rule: every surface is an ink-outlined object sitting on top of the page, and it presses down when you touch it. Tokens here map 1:1 to the Flutter MindforgeTokens ThemeExtension at the bottom of the page.

01

Personality

Loud, warm, physical. A seaside arcade cabinet rebuilt as a phone app: heavy ink outlines, hard offset shadows with no blur, saturated colour blocks, and type that grins. Nothing is flat, nothing is subtle, nothing is corporate.

Choose this when…

  • The app must feel like a toy you want to poke, not a tool you have to use
  • Sessions are short and the reward loop is the product
  • The audience skews broad and casual — family living rooms, commutes, waiting rooms
  • You want each new game to arrive with its own colour and instantly belong

Avoid this when…

  • The screen is dense with numbers — hard shadows eat vertical rhythm fast
  • The brand needs to read clinical, medical or "serious cognitive science"
  • A dark mode is required — this direction is committed to light and warm
  • Long reading sessions are expected; this is a sprint aesthetic, not a marathon one

Non-negotiables

  • 3px ink border on every surface — the border is the brand
  • Shadow offsets only on the +x/+y diagonal, never blurred, always ink
  • Press = translate toward the shadow + shrink shadow to 1px
  • Ink is the default text colour on light fills, and the only one on a saturated fill; the six answer fills declare their own label colour in §03, and nothing else may

Stroop answer palette

The whole mechanic of Stroop Rush is "which hue is this glyph painted in", so these six are the most load-bearing colours in the product. They are deliberately not the brand primitives: brand colours are tuned to feel good next to each other, answer colours are tuned to be told apart at speed and to hold contrast as text on cream.

Hue is never the only channel

Hue alone does not survive this product's own audience. Simulated under Vienot-1999, playRed #D81E2C becomes #7E7E1A and playGreen #157A39 becomes #68683C — ΔE76 27.0 apart for a deuteranope, 17.2 for a protanope, and in flat greyscale red, blue and green sit within 1.02–1.09:1 of each other. So every answer colour owns a fill pattern drawn in ink, and that pattern is painted on the answer key and into the stimulus glyph itself. Match the pattern, not the hue, and the game is still playable with no colour vision at all.

The yellow problem, and the fix

A yellow that still reads as yellow cannot reach 4.5:1 against a cream background — darken it far enough and players call it brown, which breaks the game. So the stimulus word is never bare: every stimulus carries a 3px ink outline plus an 8px ink hard shadow, which is exactly the arcade look we wanted anyway. The outline is drawn from --ink, so the effective text contrast is always ink-on-cream (14.4:1) and the hue becomes decoration on top of a legible shape. Answer buttons solve it differently again: yellow takes an ink label (8.2:1), the other five take a paper or cream label.

The Colour-blind friendly palette setting re-points the four answer slots onto playBlue, playYellow, playOrange and playPink — it does not "swap red/green for blue/orange", which would have put blue in the set twice and paired orange against red, two colours that simulate to within ΔE76 5.2 of each other. All four still clear 4.5:1 as text on cream and 4.9:1 under a paper or ink label. The fill patterns are not part of the setting: they are always on, in both palettes, because a pattern is the only channel that survives a black-and-white screenshot. In Flutter the swap is tokens.answerColour(a, colourBlind: true) — see §12.

A 4pt base with seven steps, and the app uses all of them. Screen gutter is always 20, the gap between stacked cards is always 16, and inside a card the padding is 15–17. Hard shadows already add visual space, so gaps run tighter here than in a soft-shadow system.

07

Elevation

There is no blur in this system. Elevation is an ink rectangle offset down and right — light comes from the top-left, always, and the shadow is the same ink as the border so surfaces read as die-cut card stock.

e0 · none

Pressed buttons, found tiles. Flat on the page.

e1 · 3px 3px

HUD pills, chips, small tiles, icon buttons.

e2 · 5px 5px

Cards, buttons, the default raised surface.

e3 · 8px 8px

Hero panels, stimulus card, modal sheets.

e4 · 10px 10px

One per screen, maximum: the countdown ring.

The press rule. On press, translate the surface by (offset − 1px) on both axes and shrink the shadow to 1px 1px. An e2 button moves 4px and keeps a 1px shadow, so the object appears to be pushed into the page rather than simply changing colour. Every pressable surface in the app obeys this — it is the single most recognisable behaviour in the direction.

Iconography

Same rule as everything else: one ink line, rounded ends, no fills except where a glyph is a solid shape. Two stroke weights only, and nothing in between. Icons are drawn, never borrowed — there is no emoji anywhere in the product, because a full-colour Apple or Segoe glyph is the one mark that would not look hand-built.

Motion

Fast and springy. Nothing eases gently; things arrive slightly past their mark and settle. Above 240ms this direction starts to feel sluggish and cheap, so nothing is allowed to run longer.

tap · 120msease-pop — press down / release
cubic-bezier(.2, 1.5, .4, 1)
state · 160msease-out — toggles, selection, HUD value swaps
cubic-bezier(.2, .8, .2, 1)
move · 180msease-in-out — sheets, page transitions
cubic-bezier(.6, 0, .3, 1)
celebrate · 240msease-pop — personal-best badge, streak bump
Scale 0.86 → 1.06 → 1.0, plus a 2.5° tilt. Never loops.

The four tracks above run at roughly 10× the real duration so the curve is readable at a glance; the shipped values are the ones printed on each card. Nothing in the system is allowed above 240ms, and nothing is allowed to repeat without an end — the wrong-tile shake is 240ms × 2, then rest.

Reduced motion. When the OS flag or the in-app Reduce motion switch is on, every duration collapses to 0ms and the press transform is dropped — the pressed colour and shadow state still applies instantly, so feedback survives. Rays and sunbursts stop animating; they never animated in the first place, which is deliberate.

Accessibility rules

A colour-recognition game has an obvious obligation. These are the rules that keep the direction honest.

Always true

• Body text is ink on cream or paper — 14.4:1 and 15.2:1.
• Secondary text is ink-2 on cream or paper only — 7.3:1 and 7.8:1. On any saturated fill the label goes to full ink, because ink-2 drops to 2.8:1 on coral and 3.7:1 on turquoise.
• ink-3 is disabled controls and nothing else. A status line or a tagline is ink-2.
• Every decorative ray or dot layer is re-measured as rendered pixels, not against the flat fill underneath. Worst-case measured composites: Home header ink 6.4:1, countdown cream 4.7:1, Settings header cream 5.0:1, Stroop hero ink 4.9:1, Results header ink 5.8:1, Stats header ink 5.5:1. That is why the countdown burst runs at .55 and the Stroop hero dots at .08.
• The two play-screen colour bands carry no small text at all — only ink-outlined objects sit on them — which is what lets their ray and dot layers run at full strength.
• Every fill that carries a label declares its label colour in the token table; there is no "pick one" case.
• Tap targets are ≥ 48px: icon buttons 48×48, segmented items 48 tall, answer keys 92, grid tiles 60–64. The toggle track is 66×34 and its tap target is the 62px settings row around it.
• Focus is a 4px grape-pop ring outside a 3px cream gap — implemented on buttons, cards, tiles, answers, segmented items, tabs and toggles, and shown above on sunshine, turquoise and yellow fills.
• Every answer colour carries an ink fill pattern as well as a hue, on the key and on the stimulus glyph.
• The 3px ink border means every surface boundary survives greyscale and low vision.

Never allowed

• Hue as the sole carrier of state or of an answer — pair it with shadow, ring, position, a word or a fill pattern.
• A bare coloured glyph on cream — the stimulus outline and its fill pattern are not optional.
• Element opacity on anything that has an ink border; recede by changing the fill instead.
• Text on grape-pop below 24px.
• Motion that a player cannot turn off, that runs longer than 240ms, or that repeats without an end.
• Feedback that only exists as motion.
• Disabled styling without a disabled shape change (border drops to ink-3, shadow goes soft-ink).

/// The non-hue channel every answer colour must also carry. Red and green
/// collapse to one olive under deuteranopia and to one grey in a screenshot;
/// stripe and dot never do. Painted on the answer key AND into the glyph.
enum PlayFill { solid, stripe, dot, ring }

/// The four default answers plus the two Blitz extras, each bound to its fill.
/// A widget asks for [PlayAnswer.red] and gets a colour + a pattern, never a
/// bare Color it then has to decorate by hand.
enum PlayAnswer {
  red(PlayFill.stripe),
  blue(PlayFill.solid),
  green(PlayFill.dot),
  yellow(PlayFill.ring),
  purple(PlayFill.solid),
  orange(PlayFill.stripe);

  const PlayAnswer(this.fill);

  /// The ink pattern drawn over this answer's hue.
  final PlayFill fill;
}

The stimulus outline is a three-pass paint in Flutter: draw the glyph once with a Paint()..style = PaintingStyle.stroke..strokeWidth = 6..color = tokens.border, then filled with tokens.answerColour(a, colourBlind: cvd), then a third pass clipped to the glyph that strokes the a.fill pattern in tokens.border. Those are the three most important lines of rendering code in the app — the first makes the colour game legible, the third makes it playable without colour vision. The colour-blind setting is read from settings state and passed in; no widget branches on it.
