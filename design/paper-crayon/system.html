Paper Crayon

A sketchbook come to life. Warm paper, ink outlines that wobble, crayon fills that streak, washi tape holding cards to the page. It is hand-made, not childish — the charm lives in the shapes and the handwriting, while every number, label and paragraph is set in Nunito so the data never pays for the personality.

00

Personality

MindForge is offline, account-free and free of ads. It has nothing to sell you, and Paper Crayon is the direction that makes that feel like generosity rather than austerity. It reads as a well-art-directed activity book: warm, unhurried, a little imperfect, and unmistakably not a productivity dashboard.

Choose this when…

• The app should feel like a gift or a hobby, not a discipline tool. Training your brain is voluntary; the visual language should never nag.

• You want each game to keep its own character. Wobble, rotation and crayon fills absorb wildly different board designs without the shell falling apart.

• The product is a long-lived engine. Hand-drawn shapes hide the seams when a new game lands with an odd aspect ratio or an unusual control.

• You are competing on charm rather than features, and want people to screenshot it.

Do not choose this when…

• The roadmap includes dense data — leaderboards, long histories, cohort tables. Handwriting is banned from those, so the personality thins out exactly where you need it.

• You need a dark mode. Paper Crayon is deliberately single-theme: crayon on paper inverted becomes chalk on slate, which is a different design system, not a variant.

• The audience skews clinical or corporate (cognitive assessment, clinician-facing).

• Localisation includes scripts Patrick Hand and Caveat do not cover. Plan a Nunito fallback for the display face, and accept a flatter look in those locales.

The three moves that make it Paper Crayon

1 · Wobble the outline. Every container gets a 2.5px graphite stroke and an asymmetric multi-value border-radius, plus a rotation between −1.5° and 1.5°. Never two adjacent cards at the same angle.

2 · Tape it down. Anything that matters — the Daily Mix CTA, the stimulus card, the score, the chart — is held to the page by a strip of translucent washi tape overlapping the top edge.

3 · Draw the emphasis. Underlines, circles and arrows are inline SVG squiggles in a crayon colour, never CSS borders. They are decoration only, and always aria-hidden.

Stroop answer palette

Stroop Rush is the one screen where colour is the mechanic, so these are not decoration — they are the interface. The palette splits in two, and the split is the whole point. An answer chip is a shape: it is enclosed by a 2.5px graphite outline, so WCAG 1.4.11 is satisfied by the boundary and the fill can stay as loud as a crayon. The stimulus word is text: no outline rescues a text contrast ratio, so green and yellow get their own deeper ink. The outline is load-bearing — and it is not enough on its own

Green (2.54:1) and yellow (1.77:1) do not clear 3:1 against the card they sit on. That is a real finding, not a rounding error, and it is why every chip carries a 2.5px --stroke outline: WCAG 1.4.11 is satisfied by the graphite boundary at 12.76:1, not by the fill. The outline must never be softened, removed, or reduced below 2px for "cleanliness". The same dependency applies to the timer bar, where the --danger fill sits at 2.91:1 against its --surface-deep track and is rescued only by the 2.5px graphite cap on the fill's leading edge.

Hue alone cannot separate four answers. Simulated with the Viénot transform, a deuteranope sees #E5544B as #939342 and #58B368 as #9F9F6B — 1.18:1 apart, which is to say identical. Every pair in the set lands under 3:1, and we searched: no four-hue set clears 3:1 between all six pairs under deuteranopia while staying legible on warm paper. So colour is not the channel that carries the answer. Shape and pattern are, and they are printed on the chip and on the stimulus so the match is shape-to-shape. Each chip also prints its own name in graphite on paper white at 12.76:1.

Rules for the stimulus

The stimulus is set at 104px, which is large text, so the applicable floor is 3:1 — but it is also the single most important element in the product, so it is drawn with a 2px --stroke outline as a second, independent boundary. Together that is belt and braces: the fill clears the floor on its own and the glyph has a 12.76:1 edge.

Never reuse the chip fills for the stimulus, and never use the stimulus ramp below 24px. In Flutter the two live in separate lists — stroopStimulus and stroopAnswers — precisely so they cannot be confused at a call site.

Beside the word sits the same shape badge the matching chip carries. That badge is the redundant channel: naming the ink is the task, so the stimulus is the one place this design must not rely on hue.

Why this set, and what it does not claim

The old high-contrast set swapped green → violet and yellow → graphite. That was wrong: under deuteranopia --marker-blue and --violet simulate to 1.07:1 of each other, so it traded a red/green collision for a blue/violet one. Violet is dropped from the answer set entirely.

This set does not claim hue separation, because that is not achievable. What it maximises is lightness separation — the four simulate to widely different luminances instead of four muddy olives — and it keeps every fill at or above 5:1 against the card except sunny, which is outline-carried as before. The identification still rides on shape, pattern and the printed name, which are unaffected by any colour vision deficiency.

The Settings row reads "Colour-blind palette · Swaps green and yellow, adds a shape to every cue". Copy and behaviour must stay in step: if the shapes ever ship as always-on, the subtitle changes with them.

Non-negotiables

• Every font-family declaration ends in a real fallback stack — cursive for the two hands, system-ui for Nunito. Bundle all three in the Flutter app; never fetch them at runtime.

• Numerals are always tabular. A HUD that jitters as the score ticks is a bug.

• Handwriting never goes below 17px. That is a hard floor with a name — the sticker step above is the smallest handwritten size in the system. It also never sets more than about four words, never carries an error message, and never sets a paragraph or a data value: chart values, stat units, list-row titles and anything you would put in a table are Nunito. If a string can grow with localisation, it belongs to Nunito too.

• These are the only sizes, and they govern everything inside a phone screen. (This reference page and the gallery are documentation around the product and use a wider web scale on purpose — do not transcribe their sizes into the app.) Nunito: 10 · 12 · 13 · 15 · 18. Patrick Hand: 17 · 20 · 23 · 26 · 34 · 50 · 76 · 104. Caveat: 36 · 52 · 190. There are no fractional steps — a 12.5px or a 14.5px in a diff means someone eyeballed it, and the reviewer will find it before you do.

• Respect the OS text-scale setting up to 200%. Every screen in this system is laid out so the handwriting can grow without clipping — the Stroop stimulus scales, it does not shrink to fit.

05

Radius

Every radius is asymmetric on both axes. That is the whole trick: a symmetric radius reads as machined, a multi-value one reads as drawn. Pair each with a rotation between −1.5° and 1.5° and never repeat the same radius twice in a vertical stack — alternate --r-card and --r-card2.

r-tile 10px 14px 11px 13px / 13px 11px 14px 10px Grid tiles, HUD pills, icon squares, small wells.
r-card 20px 26px 18px 24px / 24px 18px 26px 20px The default container. Odd-indexed cards in a list.
r-card2 26px 18px 24px 20px / 18px 26px 20px 24px The mirror. Even-indexed cards, so no two neighbours match.
r-blob 30px 12px 28px 14px / 14px 28px 12px 30px Buttons and the segmented control. Reads like a drawn capsule.
r-leaf 255px 15px 225px 15px / 15px 225px 15px 255px The signature blob. Large decorative shapes only — at chip size the browser scales it back to a plain rectangle.
r-stamp 17px 6px 15px 8px / 8px 15px 6px 17px The small-element version of r-leaf. Stickers, badges, counters.
r-pill 999px Only where a true capsule is functional: progress tracks, toggle bodies, the home indicator.
06

Elevation

Paper Crayon does not blur. Depth is a hard offset shadow — the shadow a cut-out casts on the page below — with at most a small soft halo underneath it. A blurred Material elevation instantly makes the whole thing look like a filter applied to a normal app.

Dashed + lift-0 means inert. Nothing else may borrow it.

A dashed --stroke-soft outline with no shadow is the system's word for "you cannot act on this": the locked N-Back card, the grouping strip, a found tile. Live personal data — lifetime tallies, personal bests, anything the player earned — always gets a solid --stroke and a lift-1. Drawing someone's own numbers in the disabled-slot language is the fastest way to make a working screen look broken.

lift-0none Flush with the page. Reserved for things that are genuinely inert: the locked game card, the "this week" grouping strip, a grid tile already found.
lift-10 2px 0 ink-a18 Resting: HUD pills, stickers, grid tiles, small chips.
lift-20 3px 0 ink-a28, 0 8px 16px -8px The standard card. Game cards, list containers, the chart.
lift-30 5px 0 ink-a45, 0 14px 24px -12px Hero: Daily Mix, the Stroop stimulus, the score card, modal sheets.
stamp0 4px 0 ink-a70 Buttons only. On press the shadow collapses to 0 and the button drops 4px — it gets stamped into the paper.
07

Motion

Three durations, two curves, one idea: things get stamped into place. Nothing slides in from off-screen and nothing fades — a crayon either is on the paper or it is not. Under Reduce motion every duration collapses to 0ms and the end state is identical, so motion is never the thing carrying the meaning.

quick150ms · ease-out State flips you should not notice: press feedback, toggle knob, segment selection.
base200ms · ease-out Content changes: a new Stroop word, a tile turning found, a HUD value stepping.
stamp250ms · ease-stamp Arrivals worth celebrating: the countdown numeral, the score card, a new-best badge.
ease-outcubic-bezier(.22,.8,.3,1) The workhorse. Leaves fast, settles gently, never overshoots. Use it unless you have a reason not to.
ease-stampcubic-bezier(.2,1.5,.4,1) Overshoots ~8% then settles. Reserved for arrivals and press-release. Two stamps on screen at once is one too many.
Haptics ride alongside

Every stamp fires a light impact on the commit frame — once, never per animation frame. Correct answer: light. Wrong answer: light double-tap, never heavy. New personal best: medium, exactly one. All of it behind the single Haptics switch in Settings, and all of it redundant with sound and a visual change so that turning it off loses nothing.

Flutter mapping

Drop this into lib/theme/mindforge_tokens.dart. It is the same two-tier structure as the CSS: private primitives at the top, public semantic slots below, and a MindforgeTokens.of(context) that asserts rather than returning null. Once it is in place, no widget file should contain a raw Color, Duration, Curve or BorderRadius — the same discipline this page enforces by keeping every hex inside :root.

  @override

      surface: c(surface, other.surface),
      surfaceRaised: c(surfaceRaised, other.surfaceRaised),
      surfaceSunk: c(surfaceSunk, other.surfaceSunk),

      successInk: c(successInk, other.successInk),
      warning: c(warning, other.warning),
      warningInk: c(warningInk, other.warningInk),
      danger: c(danger, other.danger),
      dangerInk: c(dangerInk, other.dangerInk),
      focusRing: c(focusRing, other.focusRing),
      stroke: c(stroke, other.stroke),
      onAccent: c(onAccent, other.onAccent),
      stroopAnswers: t < 0.5 ? stroopAnswers : other.stroopAnswers,
      stroopStimulus: t < 0.5 ? stroopStimulus : other.stroopStimulus,
      space: t < 0.5 ? space : other.space,
      strokeWidth: strokeWidth + (other.strokeWidth - strokeWidth) * t,
      durQuick: t < 0.5 ? durQuick : other.durQuick,
      durBase: t < 0.5 ? durBase : other.durBase,
      durStamp: t < 0.5 ? durStamp : other.durStamp,
    );
  }

  List<Object?> get _props => <Object?>[

    if (other is! MindforgeTokens) return false;
    final a = _props, b = other._props;
    for (var i = 0; i < a.length; i++) {
      if (a[i] != b[i]) return false;
    }
    return true;
  }

/// The single place ThemeData is built. Fonts are bundled assets — never
/// fetched at runtime, because MindForge must work with the radio off.
/// Requires Flutter >= 3.22: `background` and `onBackground` were required
/// named arguments of the const [ColorScheme] constructor until they were
/// deprecated there. On 3.16–3.21 add `background: _paper, onBackground:
/// _graphite,` below with an `// ignore: deprecated_member_use`.
ThemeData buildPaperCrayonTheme() {
  const t = MindforgeTokens.light;
  const scheme = ColorScheme(
    brightness: Brightness.light,
    primary: _violet,       onPrimary: _paperWhite,
    secondary: _markerBlue, onSecondary: _paperWhite,
    error: _crayonRed,      onError: _paperWhite,
    surface: _paper,        onSurface: _graphite,
  );

  return ThemeData(
    useMaterial3: true,
    colorScheme: scheme,
    scaffoldBackgroundColor: t.surface,
    fontFamily: 'Nunito',
    extensions: const [t],
    textTheme: const TextTheme(
      // display  — Caveat, once per screen
      displayLarge:  TextStyle(fontFamily: 'Caveat', fontWeight: FontWeight.w700, fontSize: 44, height: 1.00),
      // numeral-xl — Patrick Hand, the one number a screen exists for
      displayMedium: TextStyle(fontFamily: 'PatrickHand', fontSize: 76, height: 0.95),
      // title      — Patrick Hand, short strings only
      titleLarge:    TextStyle(fontFamily: 'PatrickHand', fontSize: 26, height: 1.15),
      // numeral    — HUD values, tabular
      titleMedium:   TextStyle(fontFamily: 'PatrickHand', fontSize: 23, height: 1.13),
      // heading / body / label / caption / micro — Nunito, always
      titleSmall:    TextStyle(fontWeight: FontWeight.w800, fontSize: 18, height: 1.33),
      bodyLarge:     TextStyle(fontWeight: FontWeight.w600, fontSize: 15, height: 1.47),
      bodyMedium:    TextStyle(fontWeight: FontWeight.w700, fontSize: 13, height: 1.38),
      bodySmall:     TextStyle(fontWeight: FontWeight.w600, fontSize: 12, height: 1.33),
      labelSmall:    TextStyle(fontWeight: FontWeight.w900, fontSize: 10, height: 1.20, letterSpacing: 1.4),
    ),
  );
}
How to keep it honest in the codebase

• Add a policy test that greps lib/ outside lib/theme/ for Color(0x, Colors., Duration(, Curves. and fontSize:. If it matches, the build fails. That test is the whole reason this token layer survives contact with a deadline.

• Bundle Caveat, PatrickHand and Nunito in pubspec.yaml. Do not use google_fonts — an offline app must not have a network dependency in its first frame.

• Read reduce-motion from MediaQuery.disableAnimationsOf(context) as well as the in-app switch, and collapse all three durations to Duration.zero. The end state must be pixel-identical — put the resting transform on the widget, never in the last keyframe, or turning motion off also turns the tilt off.

• The mockups in app.html are fixed 390×844 boxes with overflow:hidden; the build is not. No MediaQuery.withClampedTextScaling, no FittedBox and no TextOverflow.ellipsis to make the Stroop stimulus or a Settings row fit — the board reflows at the largest system text scale, it does not clip. The design-review sweep includes a largest-text-scale pass on Stroop and Settings specifically.

• Add stroopStimulus to the same policy test as the raw-colour grep: any widget that reads stroopAnswers to paint the stimulus word, or stroopStimulus to paint a chip, is a contrast bug that no linter will catch on its own.

Paper Crayon is one of three directions for MindForge. It is the one that trades a little data density for a lot of warmth — pick it if you want people to feel invited, not measured.
