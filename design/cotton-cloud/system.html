MindForge · Design direction 01

Cotton Cloud

Soft, calm, breathable cheer. Candy colour with grown-up manners — the brain gym you open to unwind, not to compete. Layers float on a lilac-white page, blurred colour drifts behind frosted cards, and every corner is pillowy. Nothing shouts, including the win screen.

When to choose this direction

Choose Cotton Cloud when

  • The product promise is restorative — train to feel clearer, not to beat someone.
  • Sessions are short and repeated daily; the app must never feel like a chore.
  • You want a platform look that survives ten more games without a re-skin.
  • The audience skews broad and adult, and loud gamification would repel them.

Choose something else when

  • You need arcade adrenaline — Cotton Cloud has no gear for shouting.
  • Screens must carry dense tables or many simultaneous numbers.
  • The device fleet is low-end: blur, frost and wide shadows cost fill-rate.
  • Brand requires a dark mode as a first-class surface, not an afterthought.

Why the stimulus is drawn in ink, not pure. A pure tone only clears 3:1 on white for red; green and yellow sit at 2.05 and 1.58. If the giant word were drawn in pure, a Stroop round that happened to pick yellow would be unreadable. So the rule is absolute: colour words are always ink (5.84–9.03:1), and pure tones are only ever solid non-text blocks, where hue — not luminance — carries identity. Hue is shared between the stimulus and its matching chip, which is how people categorise colour anyway.
Redundant channels. Adjacent pure tones separate by as little as 1.02:1 in luminance, so colour alone can never be the answer. Three channels ship unconditionally: every answer button carries its name in text, a shape silhouette (circle / square / triangle / diamond / hexagon / star) and a ring in the family's ink — the exact tone the stimulus word is drawn in. The Colour-blind friendly palette setting adds the matching shape as a corner mark on the stimulus panel. Yellow and orange ink were deepened to #6B4A00 and #7A3410 so red, yellow and orange separate by lightness, the one channel dichromacy leaves intact — under deuteranopia the old #8A5D00 and #B0203D simulated to the same olive at 1.06:1.

Space & radius

A 4-point spine, and it binds component interiors as tightly as it binds block rhythm: there is no 14, 15, 18 or 13 px padding anywhere in the screens. Screen gutter is the single named exception at 22 px, so a 5-column Schulte grid lands on whole pixels at 390 pt. Radii start high and go higher — the smallest corner in the system is 10 px, and nothing anywhere has a border.

06 — Foundations

Motion

Things fade and drift; nothing snaps. Three durations, three curves, and a hard rule that motion may decorate a state change but never be the state — every screen is identical and complete with animation switched off.

Drift out

240ms · cubic-bezier(.22, 1, .36, 1)

The default. Anything appearing, growing or settling: cards on entry, sheet presentation, the found-tile fill.

Gentle overshoot

300ms · cubic-bezier(.34, 1.18, .48, 1)

Reserved for reward: the personal-best badge, a streak increment, the countdown numeral. A 18 % overshoot — enough to feel alive, not enough to bounce.

Symmetric

160ms · cubic-bezier(.65, 0, .35, 1)

Two-way state that must feel instant and reversible: toggle knob, segmented selection, press-down scale. The only place 160 ms is allowed.

Reduce motion. With the setting on — or the OS flag set — background blobs freeze, the next-tile breathe stops, and every duration collapses to zero. The blobs remain visible as static colour, so the screen never loses its identity; only the movement goes.

Rules of thumb

Do let blobs touch the edgesA blob must be clipped by the screen bounds. A fully visible circle reads as a graphic; a clipped one reads as atmosphere.
Don't frost without a blobFrosted glass over flat paper is just a slightly grey card. Frost is only earned where something colourful is behind it.
Do keep one gradient per screenThe Daily Mix card, the Start button, or the score — never two. The gradient is the loudest voice in the system.
Don't add a border to fix contrastThere are no borders. If an edge is unclear, raise elevation or deepen the fill by one tone.
Do give every colour state a wordFound tiles, wrong taps and answer buttons all carry text or a glyph. Colour is a second channel, never the first.
Don't let a game restyle the chromeGames own the board area only. HUD, nav, sheets and buttons are the platform's, so game eleven costs no design.
Do give a board its own fieldA grid of tiles floating on bare paper reads unfinished. Put it on a frosted panel with the rule printed underneath, and put a blob behind the panel so the frost is earned.
Don't fill a track with a pastelA base tone is 1.4:1 against haze. Fills, ring strokes and chart bars use the deep tones; the pastels stay where they belong, as surfaces.

import 'dart:ui' show FontFeature;

import 'package:flutter/material.dart';

/// The non-colour channel that makes a Stroop answer identifiable.
///
/// Rendered as the chip's silhouette on every answer button, always, and as a
/// corner mark on the stimulus panel when the colour-blind setting is on.
/// Shape is the only channel that survives dichromacy intact.
enum StroopShape { circle, square, triangle, diamond, hexagon, star }

/// One Stroop answer colour, in the only three tones the game may use.
///
/// The tones are not interchangeable: [soft] fills, [pure] identifies, and
/// [ink] is the sole tone permitted to be text.
@immutable
class StroopSwatch {
  const StroopSwatch({
    required this.soft,
    required this.pure,
    required this.ink,
    required this.shape,
  });

  /// Pillowy button fill. Contrast against [ink] is at least 4.9:1.
  final Color soft;

  /// The identity chip: [pure] fill, [ink] outline, [shape] silhouette.
  /// Non-text: identified by hue and form, never read.
  final Color pure;

  /// Label on [soft] and the giant stimulus word. At least 5.8:1 on white.
  final Color ink;

  /// Redundant, non-colour identity. See [StroopShape].
  final StroopShape shape;

  StroopSwatch lerp(StroopSwatch other, double t) => StroopSwatch(
        soft: Color.lerp(soft, other.soft, t)!,
        pure: Color.lerp(pure, other.pure, t)!,
        ink: Color.lerp(ink, other.ink, t)!,
        shape: t < 0.5 ? shape : other.shape,
      );

  @override
  bool operator ==(Object other) =>
      identical(this, other) ||
      other is StroopSwatch &&
          other.soft == soft &&
          other.pure == pure &&
          other.ink == ink &&
          other.shape == shape;

  @override
  int get hashCode => Object.hash(soft, pure, ink, shape);
}

/// Gameplay palette. Stroop Rush draws four per round; Blitz draws six.
@immutable
class StroopPalette {
  const StroopPalette({
    required this.red,
    required this.blue,
    required this.green,
    required this.yellow,
    required this.purple,
    required this.orange,
  });

  final StroopSwatch red;
  final StroopSwatch blue;
  final StroopSwatch green;
  final StroopSwatch yellow;
  final StroopSwatch purple;
  final StroopSwatch orange;

  /// Every swatch in deal order. Index is stable across builds so a seeded
  /// round replays identically.
  List<StroopSwatch> get all => [red, blue, green, yellow, purple, orange];

  StroopPalette lerp(StroopPalette other, double t) => StroopPalette(
        red: red.lerp(other.red, t),
        blue: blue.lerp(other.blue, t),
        green: green.lerp(other.green, t),
        yellow: yellow.lerp(other.yellow, t),
        purple: purple.lerp(other.purple, t),
        orange: orange.lerp(other.orange, t),
      );

  @override
  bool operator ==(Object other) =>
      identical(this, other) ||
      other is StroopPalette &&
          other.red == red &&
          other.blue == blue &&
          other.green == green &&
          other.yellow == yellow &&
          other.purple == purple &&
          other.orange == orange;

  @override
  int get hashCode => Object.hash(red, blue, green, yellow, purple, orange);
}

/// Registration. Call once, in the composition root.
///
/// Manrope is the default family; every heading and every number is overridden
/// to Quicksand here, so no widget has to remember which face it wants.
ThemeData cottonCloudTheme() {
  const tokens = MindforgeTokens.cottonCloud;
  const head = 'Quicksand';
  const displayXl = TextStyle(
    fontFamily: head,
    fontWeight: FontWeight.w700,
    fontSize: 76,
    height: 1,
    letterSpacing: -2.66,
    fontFeatures: [FontFeature.tabularFigures()],
  );
  return ThemeData(
    useMaterial3: true,
    scaffoldBackgroundColor: tokens.surface,
    fontFamily: 'Manrope',
    colorScheme: const ColorScheme.light(
      surface: Color(0xFFFBF7FF),
      onSurface: Color(0xFF4A3F5C),
      primary: Color(0xFF5B44C9),
      onPrimary: Color(0xFFFFFFFF),
      secondary: Color(0xFF0E7A5F),
      error: Color(0xFFB0203D),
    ),
    textTheme: const TextTheme(
      // Countdown numeral.
      displayLarge: TextStyle(
        fontFamily: head, fontWeight: FontWeight.w700, fontSize: 104, height: 1, letterSpacing: -4.16),
      // Final score.
      displayMedium: displayXl,
      headlineLarge: TextStyle(
        fontFamily: head, fontWeight: FontWeight.w700, fontSize: 30, height: 1.18, letterSpacing: -0.45),
      headlineMedium: TextStyle(
        fontFamily: head, fontWeight: FontWeight.w700, fontSize: 22, height: 1.30, letterSpacing: -0.22),
      titleLarge: TextStyle(fontFamily: head, fontWeight: FontWeight.w600, fontSize: 17, height: 1.35),
      // Every HUD value. Tabular figures stop the clock jittering.
      titleMedium: TextStyle(
        fontFamily: head,
        fontWeight: FontWeight.w700,
        fontSize: 19,
        height: 1.15,
        fontFeatures: [FontFeature.tabularFigures()],
      ),
      bodyLarge: TextStyle(fontWeight: FontWeight.w500, fontSize: 16, height: 1.55),
      bodyMedium: TextStyle(fontWeight: FontWeight.w500, fontSize: 15, height: 1.55),
      bodySmall: TextStyle(fontWeight: FontWeight.w500, fontSize: 13.5, height: 1.5),
      // Small-caps label. 11 is the floor; nothing is set below it.
      labelSmall: TextStyle(fontWeight: FontWeight.w800, fontSize: 11, height: 1.4, letterSpacing: 1.43),
    ),
    extensions: const <ThemeExtension<dynamic>>[tokens],
  );
}
Bundle the fonts. Quicksand and Manrope ship in assets/fonts/ and are declared in pubspec.yaml — never fetched with google_fonts. MindForge has no network permission at all, and a font that silently falls back would break every number in the HUD.
