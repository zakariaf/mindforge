MindForge · Design direction review

Three ways MindForge could look.

MindForge is an offline brain-training app built in Flutter. No accounts, no sign-in, no network permission at all — every score, streak and setting lives on the device. It ships as a hub that hosts many small games: Stroop Rush and Schulte Grid are built, a Daily Mix stitches them together, and the roadmap is more games, not deeper ones.

Three complete design directions have been drawn against the same eight screens — home hub, game detail, countdown, both games, results, stats and settings. Each one is a finished system with its own tokens, type scale, motion rules and accessibility work, not a mood board.

Flutter · one codebase Offline · no network permission No accounts 8 screens mocked per direction 2 games shipped, more queued Light only · none has dark mode

The decision on this page

Pick one direction to build. All three are production-ready as specifications and all three are internally accessible; they differ in what they make the product feel like, what they cost to render on a cheap Android device, and how gracefully they absorb game number seven. Compare below, then read the recommendation at the bottom — it argues for one and says plainly what choosing it throws away.

01 — The three directions

Each card is drawn in its own system.

The border weight, corner radius, shadow language and typeface on every card below are taken straight from that direction's tokens, so the card itself is a preview. Every hex shown is the real value from the direction's :root — nothing here is an approximation.

Direction 01

Sunburst Pop

Arcade-cabinet joy. Every surface is an ink-outlined object sitting on top of the page — 3px border, hard offset shadow, no blur — and it visibly presses down when you touch it.

Type pairing
Fredoka Display · 600/700
Nunito Body · 700/800
Best for
  • A toy you want to poke, not a tool you have to use — short sessions, reward loop as the product.
  • Broad, casual audiences: living rooms, commutes, waiting rooms.
  • Shipping games often — each takes a colour and a board, the shell never changes.
  • Surviving a 2-second store screenshot or the back row of a room.
Watch out for
  • Data density: 3px borders and hard shadows eat vertical rhythm. Stats is already the tightest of the eight screens.
  • Loudness fatigue — great at 4 minutes, tiring at 40. There is no quiet register.
  • Fredoka blurs below ~12px, so every micro-label is uppercase and tracked, forever.
  • Any new gameplay colour must clear 4.5:1 on cream and claim an unused ink fill pattern.

Direction 02

Cotton Cloud

Soft, calm, breathable cheer. Candy colour with grown-up manners — blurred colour blobs drift behind frosted, pillowy cards, every layer floats, and there is not a single border anywhere in the system.

Palette · real tokens

#B9A7FFLavender · accent
#7FE3C4Mint · success

#8FD3FFSky · info
#FFD6E8Blush · streak
#5B44C9Lav ink · text
Type pairing
Quicksand Headings + numerals · 500–700
Manrope Body · 400–800
Best for
  • A restorative promise — train to feel clearer, not to beat a leaderboard.
  • Short daily sessions the app must never feel like a chore to open.
  • A platform look that hosts ten more games without a re-skin: the chrome is entirely shared.
  • A broad adult audience that loud gamification would push away.
Watch out for
  • Blur and frost cost fill-rate. Drifting blobs plus BackdropFilter is real GPU work on cheap Android.
  • Low contrast is one careless step away — it already shipped once at 2.83:1. This palette has room for two text tones, not three.
  • Low-energy by design: no shouting register at all. A competitive ladder would feel limp, not calm.
  • 32–52px shadow blur reads as padding, so specs that look right build cramped.

Direction 03

Paper Crayon

A sketchbook come to life. Warm paper, ink outlines that wobble, crayon fills that streak, washi tape holding cards to the page — hand-made without being childish, because every number and paragraph is still set in Nunito.

#E5544BCrayon red
#3D7DD8Marker blue

Type pairing
Caveat Display hand · once per screen
Patrick Hand Labels + HUD · 400
Nunito Everything you read · 400–900
Best for
  • An app that should feel like a gift or a hobby, not a discipline tool. Nothing nags.
  • Letting each game keep its own character — wobble and rotation absorb wildly different boards.
  • A long-lived engine: hand-drawn shapes hide the seams when game #7 arrives with an odd aspect ratio.
  • Making "offline and free" read as warmth rather than as a missing feature.
Watch out for
  • Handwriting hurts dense data — Caveat and Patrick Hand are banned from paragraphs and tables, so data screens drift to plain Nunito.
  • The 2.5px graphite outline is load-bearing for WCAG 1.4.11 on two Stroop chips. "Cleaning it up" breaks the core mechanic.
  • Caveat and Patrick Hand have thin script coverage — Cyrillic, Greek, Arabic and CJK go flat.
  • Wobble needs discipline: rotations only −1.5°…1.5°, never two adjacent cards at the same angle.

02 — Side by side

Where they actually differ.

All three clear WCAG on text and non-text contrast, all three ship a colour-blind-safe gameplay palette with a redundant shape or pattern channel, and none of them has a dark mode. Everything below is a real difference, not a preference.

03 — Recommendation

If MindForge has to ship many small games from one codebase.

Build this one

Ship Sunburst Pop.

The brief is a platform, not a title: two games today, more later, one Flutter codebase, one small team, and a device floor that includes cheap Android. Sunburst Pop is the direction that costs the least to render, the least to extend, and the least to get wrong — and it is the only one whose identity survives a new game arriving without anyone touching the shell.

Why it wins the platform argument
1

The entire look is one BoxDecoration.

A border plus a zero-blur offset shadow is the cheapest decoration Flutter has — no BackdropFilter, no tiled texture, no CustomPainter, no shader. On a floor-spec Android device the chrome costs effectively nothing, which leaves the whole frame budget for the game board, the only part that actually differs per game.

2

A new game is one colour and one board.

The identity is carried by the border-and-shadow grammar, not by any particular hue. Stroop Rush takes coral, Schulte takes turquoise, Daily Mix takes grape, and game #7 takes whatever is unclaimed — with no change to the hub, the nav, the results screen or the theme.

3

The contrast headroom is already banked.

Ink at 14.4:1 on cream means a new board colour is legible against the chrome before anyone measures it. Cotton Cloud has room for exactly two text tones and proved it by shipping a 2.83:1 one; that is a contrast lint you would have to build and re-run for every new game.

4

Two fonts, and neither is banned from anything.

Paper Crayon needs three families with two of them barred from data — so every new game's stats screen quietly drifts back to plain Nunito and the personality thins out exactly where the roadmap adds screens. Sunburst's two faces work everywhere in the app.

What you give up
  • The quiet gear. There is no calm register in this system. It is exhilarating for four minutes and tiring for forty, so a long-form or meditative mode has nothing to shift into. Cotton Cloud does, and that is a real loss if the roadmap goes that way.
  • Data density. Leaderboards and 30-day histories need a quieter card variant — drop to the e1 elevation, thinner dividers — designed before you need it. Stats is already the tightest of the eight screens.
  • The adult read. Cotton Cloud is the direction you put in front of a clinical or corporate buyer. Sunburst reads as a toy, which is exactly what makes it work on a store listing and exactly what loses that room.
  • Charm you can't buy back later. Paper Crayon is the one people screenshot and send to a friend. Sunburst wins attention; it does not win affection, and that gap does not close with polish.
  • The cheapest path to dark mode. None of the three has one, but Sunburst is the most committed to light-and-warm — inverting it means re-deriving ink as a warm off-white and shadows as deeper plum, which is a second system, not a toggle.
  • Small-type freedom. Fredoka goes mushy below ~12px, so every micro-label stays uppercase with +14% tracking permanently. One contributor setting an 11px sentence-case label is a visible regression.
Honest tie-breakers. If the product promise turns out to be restorative rather than rewarding — long calm sessions, an adult audience that gamification repels — pick Cotton Cloud and budget the GPU fallback on day one. If the pitch is charm and word of mouth, and you can absorb a flatter look in non-Latin locales, pick Paper Crayon and treat the load-bearing graphite outline as untouchable.
